The Process

How the Ledger Works

From the moment a government system touches a data record to the moment that record is retired, Triage AI maintains a continuous, tamper-evident log of every interaction.

01
Ingestion

Data events are captured at the source

Integration points at agency data systems emit structured events whenever a record is accessed, modified, shared, or flagged. These events are signed at origin and forwarded to the Triage AI ingestion layer in real time.

No raw personal data enters the ledger — only metadata about the interaction: who, what system, what action, what time, under what authority.

02
Classification

AI labels every event by intent and authority

Each ingested event passes through a classification pipeline that assigns intent labels (enforcement, administrative, research, undisclosed), maps the acting authority, and cross-references against declared use policies.

Events that don't match any declared policy are automatically flagged for human review and added to the anomaly queue.

03
Ledger Entry

Events are written to the immutable chain

Classified events are committed to an append-only cryptographic ledger. Each entry includes the event payload, classification result, confidence score, and a hash of the previous entry — making silent modification mathematically detectable.

04
Scoring

Agencies receive live compliance scores

Triage AI aggregates ledger entries by agency and computes running compliance scores based on three factors: disclosure obligation fulfillment, retention policy adherence, and consent record completeness. Scores update in near real-time as new events are logged.

05
Oversight Access

Authorized parties can query the ledger directly

Registered oversight bodies, credentialed journalists, and legal representatives with standing can issue structured queries against the ledger. Results are returned as auditable exports that include the underlying event chain, not just summaries.

06
Public Surface

Sanitized summaries are published openly

Aggregated, non-sensitive summaries of ledger activity are published to a public-facing dashboard. Any citizen can see how many events were logged by agency, what percentage were flagged, and how compliance scores are trending — without exposing sensitive operational details.
